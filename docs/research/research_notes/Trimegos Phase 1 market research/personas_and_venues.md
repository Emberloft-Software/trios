# Colombo target segments and venue types: evidence for Trimegos personas

Scope: facts on routine, budget, phone/data and safety context for Trimegos player segments (students, young professionals, people new to Colombo, returning diaspora, expats, women) and on venue economics (futsal, badminton, board-game cafes, gyms, cafes), Colombo first.

Conventions used in these notes:
- All sources accessed 2026-10-31 unless stated otherwise.
- USD shown at LKR 331 = USD 1 (the brief's rate), rounded.
- "[snippet]" means the figure came from a search-engine result summary and the page itself was not opened and checked. Check these before quoting them in a final deliverable.
- "OLD" marks data from before 2024.

## 1. Income and spending: salaries, student allowances, rent, cafe and leisure prices

### Takeaway
The average net monthly salary in Colombo is about LKR 84,400 (about USD 255), according to Numbeo's crowd-sourced figure updated 28 Oct 2026. Junior IT salaries are roughly LKR 80,000 to 180,000 a month. A state-university student on Mahapola gets LKR 10,000 a month (about USD 30). A chain-cafe cappuccino costs LKR 830 to 1,080 (USD 2.50 to 3.30), so a single cafe visit is about 1 to 1.3% of an average monthly net salary and about a tenth of a monthly Mahapola payment. Price is a real constraint for students, and gigs that split costs (court hire, shared plates) suit them.

### Cited Findings
**Salaries**
- Numbeo (Colombo, last updated 28 Oct 2026, 94 contributors and 860 entries in the past 12 months): average monthly net salary after tax is LKR 84,404 (about USD 255). — [Numbeo Colombo](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Colombo)

### Inferences
- The ITPro bands are almost certainly monthly. As annual figures they would be below Numbeo's monthly average salary, which is implausible for software engineers. A working assumption for a fresh IT graduate in Colombo is about LKR 80,000 to 180,000 gross a month (USD 240 to 545). Treat that as a planning range, not a fact.
- Budget tiers for personas, inferred from the figures above:
  - State-university student on Mahapola: about LKR 10,000 a month of stipend. A LKR 1,080 cappuccino is 11% of it. These students will prefer free or split-cost gigs (beach walks, campus futsal, kottu).
  - Private-university student (SLIIT, NSBM, IIT, APIIT): likely more family support, but no data was found.
  - Young IT professional: can afford weekly cafe and court gigs.
- A boarder in Colombo paying LKR 12,000 to 30,000 for a room has little space at home to host people, which supports public-venue gigs.

### Gaps
- No SLASSCOM, Hays or CB/Bankers' association data on graduate starting salaries for IT, BPO, banking or apparel HQs. Search found no SLASSCOM compensation survey. Banking and apparel graduate starting pay is **unfound**.
- No dated data on private-university student allowances or spending, and no Department of Census and Statistics (DCS) HIES breakdown for young adults in Colombo.
- Barista and The Coffee Bean & Tea Leaf (Sri Lanka) prices were not found. Only Java Lounge was verified.
- No source gives the "typical spend on a night out" for Colombo youth.
- Rent listings are undated asking prices, not transacted rents.

## 2. Commute and time: hours, traffic, transport modes and prices

### Takeaway
Colombo commuting is congestion-heavy and mostly comes in from the suburbs, but almost all the hard data is old (2012 to 2016). Ride-hail per-km rates are around LKR 68 to 100. Buses and trains are very cheap: Numbeo puts a one-way ticket at LKR 52.50. For women, public transport carries a strong harassment risk (see section 4). Gigs near the bus and rail corridors, at times that avoid the 5 to 8 pm outbound peak, will be easier to reach.

### Cited Findings
- Numbeo (Oct 2026): one-way local transport ticket LKR 52.50 (USD 0.16); monthly pass LKR 2,020 (USD 6.10); taxi start LKR 130; taxi per km LKR 120 (USD 0.36). — [Numbeo Colombo](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Colombo)
- PickMe cut its per-km charge to Rs 68 in April 2023. The article does not say whether that is for tuk or car. OLD. — [Daily FT](https://ft.lk/front-page/PickMe-reduces-prices-further-on-km-rate-to-Rs-68/44-746967)
- In 2023 the authorities set a maximum tuk fare of Rs 100 for the first km and Rs 80 for each km after. OLD [snippet] — [Daily Mirror](https://dailymirror.lk/print/business-news/PickMe-reduces-prices-further-on-km-rate/273-256955)
- In a March 2026 First Capital analysis via Echelon, the PickMe Flex car (Wagon R) costs about Rs 100 per km. [snippet] — [Echelon](https://echelon.lk/pickme-42-cheaper/)
- About 300,000 vehicles entered Colombo daily, mostly via Kelaniya Bridge and Galle Road. Inbound peaks were 6:30 to 9:00 am and 11:30 am to 2:00 pm, and outbound 5 to 8 pm. OLD (2012). [snippet] — [Sunday Times 2012](https://www.sundaytimes.lk/121111/news/motorists-left-to-manage-without-police-to-direct-traffic-20014.html)
- Average speed on one Colombo corridor was about 2.5 to 3 km/h at peak and about 9 km/h in the evening. OLD (2016). [snippet] — [Sunday Times 2016](https://www.sundaytimes.lk/160612/business-times/work-on-the-longest-overhead-bridge-in-sri-lanka-inaugurated-196784.html)
- A JICA Colombo Metropolitan Region study: about 400,000 to 450,000 office workers commute into Colombo. OLD (the cordon data dates to 1995). [snippet] — [JICA report](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11844156_03.pdf)
- A peer-reviewed study identifies time, fuel, earnings and productivity as the main costs of congestion in the Colombo Metropolitan Area. — [Ceylon Journal / SLJOL](https://caj.sljol.info/articles/70)

### Inferences
- With Numbeo's figures, a 5 km ride-hail trip costs about LKR 500 to 600, versus about LKR 50 by bus. For students, the transport cost of a gig can be as much as the gig itself. Area-based discovery (already in `lib/geo.ts`) matters.
- Inbound commuters from the suburbs leave the city in the 5 to 8 pm peak. Weeknight gigs inside Colombo may suit people who live or board nearby, and weekend gigs may suit suburban commuters.

### Gaps
- No current (2024 to 2026) commute-time survey, Colombo vehicle count, or TomTom Traffic Index entry for Colombo was found.
- No official current PickMe or Uber tuk tariff for Colombo city (the minimum fare and per-km rate in 2026). The search found only a Yamu note that the minimum outside Colombo 1 to 15 was Rs 85 for 2 km (undated).
- Typical working hours (for example 8:30 to 5:30, shift work in BPO or IT for US/UK clients) were not found from a citable source.
- No train-fare data was collected.

## 3. Phones and data: OS share, data prices, internet and social media use

### Takeaway
Sri Lanka is overwhelmingly Android: StatCounter puts it at 84.9% of mobile OS share in September 2026. Internet reach is 59.7% of the population (13.9m users), and 40% are still offline. Facebook (9.0m ad reach), YouTube (8.8m) and TikTok (6.8m adults) dominate. Instagram is small (2.25m, 9.7% of the population). Mobile data is cheap at about LKR 20 to 33 per GB on big bundles, so a PWA's weight is less of a barrier than Android device fragmentation and low-end hardware. Women are under-represented online: 43.4% of social media identities and only 33.8% of TikTok's adult ad audience.

### Cited Findings
**OS share**
- StatCounter mobile OS share, Sri Lanka, September 2026: Android 84.92%, iOS 15.06%, Linux 0.01%. — [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/sri-lanka)
- Other StatCounter monthly snapshots in 2026 show Android at about 79% to 80% and iOS at about 20% to 21% (for example June 2026: Android 79.37%, iOS 20.6%). [snippet] The share moves several points month to month. — [StatCounter mobile-tablet](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile-tablet/sri-lanka)

- Internet users: 13.9m (59.7%), and 9.37m people offline (40.3%).

**Data package prices**

### Inferences
- With Android at 80 to 85%, Trimegos should be designed and tested first on mid- or low-end Android Chrome. PWA install and Web Push work well there. iOS (15 to 20%) needs the home-screen install path for push.
- Social-only "nonstop" bundles (WhatsApp, Facebook, TikTok) are common. Many young users may have unlimited social apps but metered "anytime" data for the open web, which a PWA uses. Keep page weight low and images compressed.
- Marketing reach: Facebook and TikTok are the mass channels. Instagram is niche (2.25m) but probably concentrated among urban, Colombo-based, higher-income youth, which is an inference with no source. TikTok's skew (66% male) and the share of women in social media identities (43%) mean women need deliberate outreach.

### Gaps
- No current Dialog prepaid price per GB (the page did not render). No current Airtel data, since Airtel merged into Bharti Airtel Lanka / Dialog in 2024, which is not verified here.
- No smartphone-penetration figure. DataReportal gives connections, not the share of devices that are smartphones.
- No data on popular phone price tiers or best-selling models in Sri Lanka.
- No WhatsApp usage figure (the report excludes it). WhatsApp is widely assumed to be the main messaging app, but that is unverified.

## 4. Women: labour force participation, mobility, safety and women-only groups

### Takeaway
Only 32.4% of Sri Lankan women are in the labour force, against 69.1% of men (DCS 2025). The one major study of harassment on public transport found about 90% of women and girls aged 15 to 35 had been sexually harassed on buses or trains (UNFPA, published 2017, OLD). Women are also a minority of social media users. A women-only or women-majority gig option, and safety features (public venues, minimum three people, verified profiles), answer a documented need. Women-only running or hiking clubs in Colombo could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- DCS Labour Force Survey Annual Bulletin 2025: female labour force participation rate 32.4% (2,958,543 women), male 69.1%, overall 49.4%. [snippet of the PDF] — [DCS LFS Annual Bulletin 2025](https://www.statistics.gov.lk/Resource/en/LabourForce/Bulletins/LFS_Annual%20Bulletin_2025.pdf); same split reported by [Ada Derana](https://adaderana.lk/news/cmu3jpy1k000f356o5dd4fmgx)
- In Q1 2026 female participation rose to 32.5% from 32.0% in Q1 2025. [snippet] — [Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Unemployment-edges-down-to-3-7-in-Q1-as-labour-force-participation-slips/44-794187)
- ILO-modelled estimates differ by age base: World Bank/ILO 30.99% (2025) [snippet] — [TheGlobalEconomy](https://www.theglobaleconomy.com/Sri-Lanka/Female_labor_force_participation/)
- ADB "Exploring the Factors Behind Low Female Labor Force Participation in Sri Lanka" (Dec 2025, 110 pp.) attributes low participation to demographic, economic and social factors. The landing page lists no specific factors, and the PDF was not read. — [ADB](https://www.adb.org/publications/female-labor-force-participation-sri-lanka)
- UNFPA study: about 90% of women and girls had faced sexual harassment on public buses and trains. The sample was 2,500 women and girls aged 15 to 35 across all nine provinces. Commissioned in 2015, findings published 2017. OLD. — [Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/Special-Report/unfpa-survey-90-of-women-have-experienced-sexual-harassment-on-public-transport-in-sri-lanka/22-600298); [UNFPA](https://aa.unfpa.org/news/ninety-cent-sri-lankan-women-endure-sexual-harassment-public-transport-unfpa-study-shows)
  - In the same study, 97% said the perpetrators were male. Half of respondents used public transport to get to work and 28% for education. 29% said harassment affected school performance and 37% said it affected work performance. [snippet] — same sources
- Women are 43.4% of social media user identities, 40.0% of Facebook ad reach, 40.1% of Instagram and 33.8% of TikTok adults (late 2025). — [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-sri-lanka)
- Gender-segregated housing is a visible norm in listings: ikman shows "girls only" and "ladies" boarding rooms in Colombo 8 and Nugegoda. [snippet] — [ikman](https://ikman.lk/en/ads/matale-22/room-annex-rentals)
- Power World gym has historically priced women's memberships below men's (ladies 1-year LKR 4,692 vs gents LKR 6,452 in a Yamu listing; another listing shows annual 7.5K, 5K for women, plus an off-peak ladies day rate of Rs 300). OLD (about 2015). [snippet] — [Yamu gyms](https://www.yamu.lk/blog/colombo-gyms)
- Mixed running communities exist. Colombo City Running (CCR) is a not-for-profit community group with voluntary participation that has sponsor partnerships. Colombo Night Run is an open, non-profit night running community that meets on Thursday evenings (per a LinkedIn profile). [snippet] — [Daily FT on CCR](https://www.ft.lk/Sports/RBNPerformancepartners-CCRonce-again/23-640832); [LinkedIn](https://lk.linkedin.com/in/dinushatharindu/recent-activity/articles/)
- Women-focused hiking found so far is commercial or one-off: Intrepid Travel's "Sri Lanka Women's Expedition" uses local women guides on the Pekoe Trail, and News 1st reported a 2023 all-female global team trek of the Knuckles range. — [Intrepid](https://www.intrepidtravel.com/en/sri-lanka/sri-lanka-womens-expedition-177341); [News 1st](https://www.newsfirst.lk/2023/04/19/global-all-female-team-trek-knuckles-in-sri-lanka-with-tv-1)

### Inferences
- A woman in Colombo who depends on buses for an evening gig faces a documented harassment risk. Daytime or early-evening gigs near ride-hail-friendly venues, and a "women-only" or "women's majority" audience setting (the existing `gender_pref`), are the most evidence-backed features for this segment.
- Gender-segregated boarding suggests family or landlord norms on women's evening movement for young women living away from home. This is an inference: no curfew data was found.
- 32% participation means many women aged 22 to 30 are not in paid work, so they have less independent income but possibly more daytime availability. This is a hypothesis to test in interviews.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on curfews, family permission norms, or what time young women in Colombo stay out. The ADB report PDF probably covers mobility and safety but was not read.
- No current (2023 to 2026) harassment survey to update the 2015 to 2017 UNFPA figure.
- No verified women-only running, hiking or social clubs in Colombo were found by name. Instagram and Facebook searches are needed (the search engine does not index them well).
- Female labour force participation for Colombo District or the Western Province specifically was not collected.

## 5. Returning diaspora and expats: numbers and channels

### Takeaway
There is no reliable current count of expats in Colombo. The best official figure is an old one of "more than 25,000 foreign professionals permitted to work", led by China and India. Sri Lanka reactivated a Digital Nomad Visa on 14 Feb 2026 and issued the first one on 26 Feb 2026, so this is a small, new and growing segment. Returning Sri Lankans have a volunteer meetup network (WorkInSriLanka), which points to an existing appetite for organised social events.

### Cited Findings
- More than 25,000 foreign professionals are permitted to work in Sri Lanka, with China and India topping the list. The article is undated and likely OLD. [snippet] — [Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/breaking-news/Over-foreign-professionals-working-in-SL/108-143131)
- More than 12,000 Chinese and Indian workers were permitted to work in Sri Lanka, per Parliament, in January 2014. OLD. [snippet] — [Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/TOP-STORY/over-12000-chinese-indian-workers-in-sri-lanka/26-244964)
- Digital Nomad Visa: reactivated 14 Feb 2026, for foreigners 18+ working remotely for foreign companies, for up to one year (renewable). One report cites a USD 500 fee. Applications go through the Department of Immigration and Emigration app or in person in Colombo. [snippet] — [News 1st](https://english.newsfirst.lk/2026/02/18/sri-lanka-launch-digital-nomad-visa); [Allwork.space](https://allwork.space/2026/02/sri-lanka-launches-digital-nomad-visa-and-longer-tourist-stays)
- The first DNV was issued on 26 Feb 2026 to a Ukrainian national and her spouse. [snippet] — [Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Sri-Lanka-issues-first-Digital-Nomad-Visa/44-788961)
- More than 17,000 overseas Sri Lankans registered on the government "Contact Sri Lanka" diaspora platform (date not verified). [snippet] — [MFA](https://mfa.gov.lk/tam/?p=33046)
- WorkInSriLanka is a volunteer-led initiative that holds quarterly meetups in Colombo for Sri Lankans who returned after working abroad, and it drew interest from the expat and local business community. OLD (2014 to 2015). [snippet] — [Sunday Times 2015](https://www.sundaytimes.lk/150531/business-times/workinsrilanka-initiative-draws-attention-of-expat-and-local-business-community-150922.html); [Daily Mirror](https://dailymirror.lk/hr-marketing/wisl-to-hold-quarterly-meeting-in-colombo-next-week/275-72453)

### Inferences
- Expats and digital nomads are likely few (thousands, not tens of thousands, in Colombo) but well-off. They pay Numbeo-level prices (cappuccino about LKR 830 to 1,080) without friction and are used to Meetup and InterNations-style apps. They are good early adopters and hosts, but too few to carry the platform. English-first copy suits them.
- Returning diaspora have the strongest "I lost my friend group" job-to-be-done, and they have local cultural fluency.

### Gaps
- No InterNations Colombo membership figure (none surfaced in search; the site is login-walled).
- No current Department of Immigration count of resident visas or work permits, and no count of DNVs issued since February 2026.
- No data on returning-migrant volumes (people coming back from abroad, as opposed to departures). The SLBFE publishes departures, which were not collected here.
- People new to Colombo from other districts: **no data found** on internal migration to Colombo (the DCS census migration tables were not collected). The ikman boarding-room listings near universities (Malabe, Nugegoda, Dehiwala) are indirect evidence that the segment exists.

## 6. Venues: futsal, badminton, board games, gyms, cafes, marketing and booking

### Takeaway
Colombo has a cluster of private futsal and indoor-cricket arenas. Hire started at about LKR 2,000 to 3,500 (USD 6 to 11) in 2022, and is likely higher now. Badminton is cheap at member clubs (about LKR 400 to 550 an hour, OLD), and newer racket formats are premium (pickleball LKR 4,000 an hour). Booking is mostly by phone and social media. Sri Lankan booking apps (Bokker, Padel House SL, Play.Book) are nascent. **No board-game cafe in Colombo could be verified by search.** Venue marketing spend data does not exist publicly.

### Inferences
- Futsal economics per player: at about LKR 2,500 to 3,500 an hour in 2022, a 5-a-side game of 10 players is about LKR 250 to 350 each, before any post-2022 price rises. That is affordable even on a Mahapola budget, so futsal is a good student gig. Venues sell whole-court hours, so an empty slot at off-peak times (weekday daytime) is lost revenue. Trimegos filling off-peak courts is the obvious B2B pitch, but it is unvalidated.
- Booking happens through calls, WhatsApp and Facebook or Instagram pages, plus a few venue-owned apps. A shared booking layer does not yet dominate, so there is room for Trimegos to be the "find people" layer rather than a booking engine.
- Cafes already pay delivery platforms (Uber Eats) commission, so they understand paying per order. Paying per group visit is a related model, but this is an inference.

## 7. Who organises and who joins

### Takeaway
No Sri Lanka-specific research on who organises community events was found. The local evidence is anecdotal: volunteer-run, not-for-profit groups (Colombo City Running, Colombo Night Run, WorkInSriLanka) run on a small core of organisers with sponsor support.

### Cited Findings
- Colombo City Running is a community-based, not-for-profit group with voluntary participation and an apparel sponsor partnership (RBN). [snippet] — [Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/Sports/RBNPerformancepartners-CCRonce-again/23-640832)
- WorkInSriLanka is volunteer-led, with quarterly Colombo meetups for returnees. OLD. [snippet] — [Daily Mirror](https://dailymirror.lk/hr-marketing/wisl-to-hold-quarterly-meeting-in-colombo-next-week/275-72453)

### Inferences
- Local groups seem to grow from a founder or volunteer core plus a sponsor. For Trimegos, hosts (organisers) are the scarcer and more valuable role, and early growth should recruit them from existing running clubs, university sports societies and futsal regulars.

### Gaps
- No academic or industry research (Sri Lankan or international) on organiser-to-joiner ratios was collected. International sources (for example Meetup's published organiser statistics, or Bumble BFF and Timeleft data) were out of scope for this pass and should be gathered by another stream if needed.
- No data on university club or society participation rates (UGC statistics on student numbers per university were also not collected; that is a gap for sizing the student segment).
