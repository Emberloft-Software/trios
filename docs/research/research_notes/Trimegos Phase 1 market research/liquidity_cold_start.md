# Marketplace liquidity, cold start, no-shows, host incentives, and Colombo geography (for 07-liquidity-cold-start.md)

Scope: evidence for minimum density, seeding tactics, host incentives and no-show reduction for Trimegos (pre-launch PWA, Colombo first, gigs of 3-16 people, chat/photos unlock when full or 2h before start, about 2 upcoming gigs visible today), plus Colombo neighbourhood and seasonality facts.

All URLs accessed 2026-10-31 unless stated. Many findings came through search-engine summaries, not full-page reads; these are marked "(search summary)" and should be treated as lower confidence than "(page read)". Nothing here is invented. Anything labelled "heuristic" or "inference" is our reasoning, not a published figure.

## 1. Liquidity frameworks and benchmarks (Chen, Rachitsky, NFX, Reforge): definitions and published thresholds

### Takeaway
The frameworks agree on the method: find the smallest self-sustaining network (an "atomic network") and make it feel full before expanding. None of them gives a universal fill-rate number. The closest thing to a published threshold for our category is Timeleft's: a city launches at 151 sign-ups (third-party report), and a city has enough depth at 50+ weekly participants. Lenny, NFX and VC guides all say to benchmark against your own trend.

### Cited Findings
- Andrew Chen (*The Cold Start Problem*): the "atomic network" is the smallest network that can set off network effects; dominant products often started in niches. (search summary) [Frog Capital review](https://frogcapital.com/think-frog/the-cold-start-problem-by-andrew-chen/); [Calv.info notes](https://calv.info/bookshelf/cold-start-problem)
- Chen's atomic-network sizes, as quoted in secondary sources: Zoom about 2-3 people, Slack 5-10 (interview quote); one summary says Slack 3 and Zoom 2 instead. For Tinder, sources give 200-500 people, and one quotes Chen as saying "they needed 500 people on a college campus". Sources disagree, so treat these as approximate. (search summary) [Dialogue Review](https://dialoguereview.com/?p=27227); [The Visile](https://thevisile.substack.com/p/january22); [Frog Capital](https://frogcapital.com/think-frog/the-cold-start-problem-by-andrew-chen/)
- Lenny Rachitsky defines fill rate as the percentage of intentful sessions that convert. TaskRabbit calls fill rate its north-star marketplace metric. The post advises benchmarking against your own past numbers and watching how liquidity moves as initiatives ship, and gives no universal target in the visible text. (search summary; the post is paywalled) [Lenny's Newsletter: the most important marketplace metrics](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-most-important-marketplace-metrics)
- Lenny's research on how marketplaces got started (secondary summary): 14 of 17 marketplaces surveyed went after supply first. Each used 12 identified supply-growth levers, but most of the benefit came from 2-3 of them. Most constrained their initial market (e.g. one city or category); Thumbtack was the exception, attributed to low-frequency, high-value transactions. (search summary; the original post URL returned 404 when fetched) [skills.cat summary of Lenny's marketplace-liquidity material](https://skills.cat/skills/liqiongyu/lenny-skills-plus/marketplace-liquidity); [KJ Labuz #75 Marketplace Liquidity](https://kjlabuz.substack.com/p/marketplaceliquidity?s=w)
- NFX (James Currier, "19 Tactics to Solve the Chicken-or-Egg Problem"): liquidity is "the probability of selling something you list, or of finding something you're looking for". Many of the tactics come down to starting narrower than the eventual market, starting with the harder-to-get side, and choosing a market small enough that it can actually feel full. (search summary; full list of 19 not retrieved) [Sharetribe write-up of Currier's talk](https://sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-conference-4-2); [NFX Marketplace Scorecard](https://www.nfx.com/post/the-nfx-marketplace-scorecard)
- A VC-oriented guide says there is no universal fill-rate benchmark and that the trend matters more than the absolute level. (search summary) [Causo: marketplace liquidity seed benchmarks](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/marketplace-liquidity-seed)
- Point Nine's explainer on marketplace liquidity also exists, but it was not read in this pass. [Point Nine: WTF is marketplace liquidity?](https://writing.pointnine.com/p/wtf-is-marketplace-liquidity)
- Timeleft is the closest analogue: dinners for strangers, sold per city. A city launched only once 151 people had signed up. The stated aims were to create scarcity and to guarantee enough people for group dinners from day one. This comes from one third-party blog and was not confirmed in founder interviews. (page read) [Startup Spells: How Timeleft solved the chicken-and-egg problem](https://startupspells.com/p/how-timeleft-solved-the-chicken-and-egg-problem)
- Timeleft's stated depth benchmark is 50+ weekly participants per city. Smaller cities "fizzled" for lack of density: users got bored dining weekly with the same roughly 10 people. After over-expanding to 320+ cities (about one new city per day), Timeleft cut back to about 200-220 to build depth first. (page read; article dated 2025-10-09) [How They Build: Inside Timeleft's journey](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)

### Inferences
- For Trimegos, the atomic network is probably one activity type in one area on one recurring time slot (e.g. "Thursday 7pm futsal, Rajagiriya"), not "Colombo". This is consistent with Chen and NFX's "small enough to feel full" advice. (Inference.)
- A heuristic translation of Timeleft's benchmark: 50 weekly participants at an average gig size of about 6 means roughly 8 filled gigs a week in the launch zone. Today's ~2 visible gigs are well below that. (Heuristic derived from the Timeleft figure above. Trimegos's average gig size is an assumption.)
- Timeleft's "same 10 people" failure mode matters here because Trimegos is also about meeting new people. Depth needs a churning pool, not just a few regulars. (Inference.)
- Suggested liquidity metrics for Trimegos, adapted from Lenny's fill-rate definition: (a) % of published gigs that reach full capacity before the 2h lock; (b) % of feed sessions where the viewer finds at least one claimable gig matching their audience filter; (c) time-to-fill. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No published numeric fill-rate threshold from Lenny, NFX or Reforge was found. Reforge material was not reached.
- The full text of Lenny's "how today's fastest-growing marketplaces got their initial supply/demand" post was not retrieved (the URL returned 404). Only secondary summaries were seen.
- The exact atomic-network numbers in Chen's book could not be checked against the book itself.

## 2. How comparable products seeded (Meetup, Timeleft, Bumble BFF, Playo, Strava clubs, Partiful, Luma, run clubs, Facebook, Tinder)

### Takeaway
Seeding was always concentrated and physical: one campus, one city, one sport, or a host who brings friends. The numbers that are public: Timeleft used a 151-sign-up gate and paid ads with no city staff. Tinder used a few hundred users per campus, recruited through in-person sorority presentations and parties that required the app. Playo grew to about 20-25k users and fewer than a dozen venue partners in year one by word of mouth. Partiful grew through invite links that work without the app. Strava reported a 59% rise in running clubs in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **Timeleft:** first international expansion was Barcelona in January 2024, opened through ads with "zero team on the ground". There were no ambassadors or city managers. Format: tables of 5 strangers, about 3 hours. Usage rose from 1.4 to 2.2 experiences per member per month after adding bar meetups, women-only dinners and running groups. Scale: 150k monthly participants and €18M ARR (Aug 2025), with 15-20k reviews a week used for quality control. (page read) [How They Build](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)
- **Timeleft:** 151-sign-up city gate. Paid per dinner or by subscription from about $26. It took 3 years and 4 pivots to earn the first euro, then 7 months to €1M MRR. (page read; third-party) [Startup Spells](https://startupspells.com/p/how-timeleft-solved-the-chicken-and-egg-problem)
- **Tinder:** Whitney Wolfe travelled to campuses (often said to start at USC), presented at sorority chapters, and got members to install the app. Co-founder Justin Mateen reportedly threw a large house party that only admitted people who had downloaded the app. Accounts differ on which campus came first (USC versus Texas campuses in Time's account). (search summary; mostly blogs) [Alexander Jarvis: Tinder doing things that don't scale](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/tinder-doing-things-that-dont-scale/); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/internet/tinder-and-bumble-are-throwing-parties-at-frat-houses); [Time on Whitney Wolfe Herd](https://time.com/5947727/whitney-wolfe-herd-bumble/)
- **Bumble BFF:** launched in 2016 as a mode inside Bumble for platonic friendships. In September-October 2024 the standalone BFF app ran a series of college bar-takeover events at six campuses (Miami, Alabama, SMU, Harvard/BU, Wisconsin, USC), per an agency case study. (search summary) [Wikipedia: Whitney Wolfe Herd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Wolfe_Herd); [Jenn Curtis Events: Bumble BFF college takeover](https://www.jenn-curtis-events.com/work/bumble-bff-college-takeover)
- **Playo (Bengaluru, launched May 2015):** "over 20,000 users in its first year, purely through word of mouth". A different account says about 25,000 users and "less than a dozen partner facilities and organizers" in year one. The CEO credits word of mouth and in-app referrals. (search summary; the two figures conflict) [YourStory 2019](https://yourstory.com/2019/01/meet-playo-new-player-going-global); [SiliconIndia](https://startup.siliconindia.com/vendor/playo-explore-sports-like-never-before-cid-3285.html)
- **Playo context:** ThePrint reports Bengaluru's badminton boom as "powered by techies, app, thousands of courts". This points to IT-worker density plus venues as the base. (headline only) [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/bengalurus-badminton-boom-powered-by-techies-app-thousands-of-courts/2864587/)
- **Partiful (founded 2019):** invites are links that guests can open without the app, shared by text, email or social. In-product sends are capped at 1,000 guests per event. Third-party analysis calls the shareable invite page its core growth loop: every party exposes non-users. (search summary) [Partiful help](https://help.partiful.com/hc/en-us/articles/26506782941467-How-many-guests-can-I-invite); [Sacra on Partiful](https://sacra.com/chat/h/ca6c792b-7d6d-42a9-ae5f-0970a819c66c/); [CNBC 2025-04-19](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/19/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple.html)
- **Run clubs / Strava:** Strava's 2024 Year in Sport report (135M+ people, 190+ countries) found a 59% increase in running clubs and 18% more runs logged in groups of 10 or more. Walking clubs also grew (sources say 52% or 54%). One summary says new running clubs grew 3.5x in the 2025 report. (search summary) [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-2024-year-in-sport-report/); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/strava-2024-fitness-report-highlights-trends/); [Fortune 2025-10-13 on Strava, Gen Z and run clubs](https://fortune.com/2025/10/13/strava-ipo-ceo-michael-martin-gen-z-running-clubs-marathons-dating-apps)

### Inferences
- Several patterns line up with Trimegos's existing features:
  - Single-use invite links (`/join/<code>`) are already a Partiful-style loop. Making the link landing page work well for non-users is a cheap growth lever. (Inference.)
  - "Host brings 2 friends, fills the rest with strangers" is the seeding unit that Tinder parties and Partiful both exploit. (Inference.)
- Timeleft shows that paid ads alone can open a city but not sustain it. Depth (50+ weekly) is what keeps a city alive, which argues for one launch zone rather than all of Colombo. (Inference.)
- The run-club boom suggests a recurring, low-skill, free activity (a weekly run or walk at a fixed spot) is a good first "anchor gig" format. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No usable data found on Meetup's early days (2002-2004), Facebook campus-by-campus thresholds, Luma community growth, or Strava club seeding numbers. Not searched in depth due to budget.
- Whether Timeleft used a waitlist gate in every city, and its cost per acquired user, are not public in the sources read.

## 3. No-show rates, deposits, reminders, reputation

### Takeaway
Free events lose about 28% of registrants (median), and small free events (10-49 people) about 37%, versus about 17% for paid events (PheedLoop, 1,070+ events, April 2026). A refundable deposit lifted show-up from 45% to 91% in one case, an anecdote. Text reminders raise attendance by roughly 11 points in healthcare trials (67.8% to 78.6%, Cochrane). For a 3-6 person gig with a hard minimum of three, a single no-show can sink it, so no-show controls are core to liquidity.

### Cited Findings
- PheedLoop Event Data Lab Report #05 (29 April 2026, 1,070+ live events with check-in data):

  | Events | Median no-show | Sample |
  |---|---|---|
  | All | ~20% (mean 29%) | 1,070+ |
  | Free | ~28% | N=389 |
  | Paid | ~17% | N=683 |
  | Small free (10-49) | ~37% | not stated |
  | Small paid (10-49) | ~17% | not stated |

  Among free events, 22% lost more than half their expected attendance, and the 25th-percentile worst free events hit 47%. PheedLoop advises planning for 70% attendance at free events. (page read; reported via an aggregator citing PheedLoop) [LeadsNow: average event no-show rate](https://leadsnow.ai/event-no-show-rate/)
- PheedLoop Report #06 addendum (Sept 2026, 195 events): paying attendees showed up 2-4 percentage points more reliably than comped attendees at the same event, and free and paid events differed by about 11 points. Paid-event no-shows stayed about 16-19% whatever the registration friction, so payment rather than friction drives the difference. (page read, via aggregator) [LeadsNow](https://leadsnow.ai/event-no-show-rate/)
- A refundable $100 deposit at a 37signals/Basecamp event: the previous free event had 84 registrations and 38 attendees (45% show rate). The deposit event had 55 registrations and 50 attendees (91%). This is a single case. (page read, via aggregator) [Skift Meetings 2026-09-02](https://meetings.skift.com/2026/09/02/the-100-experiment-that-slashed-no-show-rates-to-just-10/); [LeadsNow](https://leadsnow.ai/event-no-show-rate/)
- **Meetup-specific:** a blog says Meetup itself states only 40-50% of RSVPs show up for free events, and the author's own experience is 35-40% attendance. Another organizer reports about 40% no-shows. Meetup's original statement was not verified. (search summary) [Buckley Planet, Oct 2026](https://buckleyplanet.com/2026/10/filling-seats-how-to-get-people-to-actually-show-up/); [LinkedIn: Managing no-shows for your Meetups](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/managing-no-shows-your-meetups-networking-events-sarah-reichardt)
- **Reminders (healthcare):** a Cochrane review (2013) found text reminders raised attendance from 67.8% to 78.6% (RR 1.14, 7 trials), and text and phone reminders performed about equally (RR 0.99). (page read, via aggregator) [LeadsNow](https://leadsnow.ai/event-no-show-rate/)
- A meta-analysis (Boksmati et al. 2016, J Med Syst) is reported as OR 1.62 (95% CI 1.35-1.94) for attending with SMS reminders. Another source cites the same paper as an 8.92-point attendance increase. The two disagree and the primary paper was not checked. Hasvold (2011, 29 studies) concluded "the evidence is overwhelming that reminders have a positive effect on non-attendance". One pediatric trial found text plus voice gave 23.5% no-shows vs 38.1% for voice alone. (search summary) [PMC3188816](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3188816/); [National Elf Service](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/publication-types/randomised-controlled-trial/text-messaging-to-reduce-failed-appointments/); [PMC8288327](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8288327/)
- **Commitment devices:** in a UK community weight-management trial, a commitment contract raised attendance by 7.5% (p=0.050). An Oxford study on HIV testing found people who wanted a commitment device sometimes did worse when offered one. The effect depends on the person. (search summary) [ScienceDirect: commitment contracts and public health programmes](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214804319300734); [Oxford ORA: Appointments as a commitment device](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:9b77bbe4-7cdd-4d15-bcf4-86fb7f993016/files/sj3860787d); [Rogers et al., Commitment Devices (Harvard)](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/todd_rogers/files/commitment_devices_2.pdf)
- **Restaurant deposits:** vendor data only, not peer-reviewed. One booking-platform index reports 0.80% no-shows with deposit protection vs 2.47% without. Tock claims 0.9% with full prepayment. Other vendors say deposits alone cut no-shows by 30-50%. Treat these as directional. (search summary) [Tock](https://www.exploretock.com/join/resources/deposit-for-restaurant-reservation-booking/); [QuickTable restaurant no-show statistics 2026](https://www.quicktable.co/restaurant-no-show-statistics/); [Eat App](https://restaurant.eatapp.co/blog/restaurant-no-shows)
- **Reviews as quality control:** Timeleft collects 15-20k reviews a week for quality control. (page read) [How They Build](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)

### Inferences
- Heuristic: at a 37% small-free-event no-show rate, a 6-person gig expects about 2 no-shows. A 3-person gig (Trimegos's minimum) has a high chance of dropping below three. This argues for a minimum default capacity above 3, or a waitlist/backfill once the gig locks. (Heuristic from PheedLoop's figure. Event data may not transfer to small peer gigs, where social accountability is higher.)
- The full-gig unlock rule, with names and photos revealed to a small group, likely creates more personal accountability than an anonymous 50-person RSVP. That may push Trimegos below event-industry no-show rates, but there is no data on this. (Inference, untested.)
- Cheapest levers in evidence order: (1) reminders: push already exists; add a T-24h and T-2h "still coming?" confirm. (2) Visible attendance reputation (show-up count). (3) A small refundable deposit for selected gig types only. A deposit adds payment infrastructure and friction for a team of 3. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of no-shows in small peer-to-peer social meetups (3-16 people) was found. All event figures are from conferences/ticketing or healthcare.
- No Sri Lanka-specific no-show data found.
- No direct study of reputation badges or no-show strikes on meetup attendance was found.

## 4. Host incentives in community products

### Takeaway
Hard evidence exists only for paid-host marketplaces. Airbnb's Superhost badge is associated with more bookings and reviews (about 10-17% revenue uplift in one dissertation) but not higher prices. I found no rigorous evidence on what motivates unpaid community hosts (status, perks, free entry, revenue share).

### Cited Findings
- **Airbnb Superhost:** a Tourism Management (2017) study of Hong Kong listings found the gamified Superhost badge associated with more reviews and higher ratings. (search summary) [iris.unica.it record](https://iris.unica.it/handle/11584/277850)
- An MTSU dissertation (2021) isolating the badge effect found a negligible price increase but more reviews, and estimated a 10-17% revenue increase from more bookings. (search summary) [MTSU dissertation PDF](https://jewlscholar.mtsu.edu/bitstreams/d18d9df3-c2f3-4b6b-9a3e-0874080644ad/download)
- A University of Chicago thesis (2021): Superhosts earn significantly more, mainly through occupancy rather than price. (search summary) [UChicago Knowledge](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/3159?ln=en)
- Vendor data (IntelliHost, 2023) found only a marginal conversion gap (about 0.86% vs 1.03%), so the search-ranking benefit is unclear. (search summary; vendor) [IntelliHost](https://intellihost.co/blog/how-superhost-status-affects-bookings)
- **Playo:** fewer than a dozen partner facilities and organizers in year one. This suggests a small number of committed organizers can carry early supply. (search summary) [SiliconIndia](https://startup.siliconindia.com/vendor/playo-explore-sports-like-never-before-cid-3285.html)
- Lenny's survey: 14 of 17 marketplaces went supply-first. Indeed, for example, gave suppliers stand-alone value (a free recruiting tool). (search summary) [skills.cat summary](https://skills.cat/skills/liqiongyu/lenny-skills-plus/marketplace-liquidity)

### Inferences
- The "single-player value" lesson from Indeed applies here. Trimegos hosts already get a tool for filling their own futsal or board-game group: invite links plus a stranger top-up. Pitching to existing organisers (futsal regulars, run clubs, board-game cafes) as "fill your last 2 spots" fits a supply-first approach. (Inference.)
- Status levers (a "reliable host" badge, host show-up stats) are cheap to build. The Airbnb evidence suggests they raise trust and demand, but nothing found proves they motivate unpaid hosts. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No evidence found on Meetup organizer incentives, Eventbrite host perks, run-club leader perks, or venue-partnership (free entry / discounted court) effects. The search returned nothing usable.
- No evidence found on revenue share for unpaid community hosts.

## 5. Colombo geography: where 18-35s, universities, offices, futsal and cafes concentrate

### Takeaway
The Colombo district has 2.375M people (2024 census), with 368,810 aged 20-29, its largest 10-year band. The biggest state-university student bodies near Colombo are Sri Jayewardenepura (about 15.2k undergraduates), Kelaniya (about 14.4k), Colombo (about 11.7k) and Moratuwa (about 8.2k), from UGC 2022 figures including backlog. SLIIT's main campus is in Malabe. Futsal courts cluster in the residential suburbs (Nugegoda, Rajagiriya, Dehiwala, Maharagama) rather than the commercial core.

### Inferences
- The candidate first zone is a south-east corridor: Rajagiriya, Kotte, Nugegoda, Maharagama. Reasons:
  - It concentrates futsal venues.
  - It is close to Sri Jayewardenepura, the largest state campus.
  - It sits between the Colombo core and the fast-growing suburbs (Kaduwela/Malabe, home to SLIIT, and Homagama).

  The university locations behind this (Sri J at Gangodawila, Nugegoda) are general knowledge and were not verified in this pass. A Colombo 3/4/5/7 (cafes, offices) zone is the alternative. (Inference.)

## 6. Seasonality for launch timing

### Takeaway
Colombo's two wet periods are the south-west monsoon (May-September) and the second inter-monsoon (October-November). October-November is often the wettest, and January-February the driest. Outdoor gigs (hikes, beach, runs) are most exposed in May and October-November, which includes today (2026-10-31). Indoor formats (futsal, board games, cafes) are less affected.

### Cited Findings
- Department of Meteorology (Sri Lanka) presentation: south-west monsoon May-September, second inter-monsoon October-November. October-November is described as the period contributing maximum rainfall. (search summary) [Dayananda, Dept. of Meteorology, JMA presentation](https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/en/Activities/qmws_2010/Presentation/S404_Dayananda.pdf)
- WeatherSpark gives November as the wettest month for Colombo (average 8.7 inches) and February as the driest. Avionero calls October the wettest (about 295 mm). Red Dot Tours says May and October to mid-November are the wettest periods, and January and February the driest. The sources disagree on October vs November. (search summary) [WeatherSpark: Colombo](https://weatherspark.com/y/109720/Average-Weather-in-Colombo-Sri-Lanka-Year-Round); [Red Dot Tours: weather in Colombo](https://reddottours.com/weather-in-colombo); [Avionero: Colombo October](https://avionero.com/New-York-Colombo.NYC-CMB/weather/october)
- Official monthly rainfall statistics exist in the Department of Census and Statistics abstract (chapter 1.6) but were not read. [DCS Statistical Abstract 2021, 1.6](https://www.statistics.gov.lk/abstract2021/CHAP1/1.6)

### Inferences
- Launching now (late October 2026) means launching into the wettest stretch. Lead with indoor anchor gigs (futsal, board games, cafes) and add outdoor formats from December to January. (Inference.)
