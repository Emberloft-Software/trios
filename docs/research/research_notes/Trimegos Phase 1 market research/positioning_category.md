## 1. How comparable products describe themselves (headline, store subtitle, category)

### Takeaway
The category leaders split into two groups. **Friendship-outcome brands** (Timeleft, Meetup, Bumble For Friends, Hey! VINA, Peanut) put "friends" or "people" in the headline. **Event/plan-tool brands** (Partiful, Luma, Playo) lead with the activity or the event and never mention friendship. "IRL" works as a store-subtitle keyword (Timeleft, Bumble For Friends). Nobody in the set uses "dating" or "not dating" in a homepage headline. The anti-dating message lives in FAQs/blogs and in product mechanics ("No bios. No swiping.").

## 2. Friend apps used for dating, and the tactics products use against it

### Takeaway
Operators openly admit that romance happens. Timeleft says "Romance happens. It isn't what the table is set up for." 222 sells a "blind date" format next to its friend formats. The common defences are structural: groups instead of 1:1s, public venues, personality matching, curation/vetting, selfie or ID verification, and single-gender spaces (Peanut, Hey! VINA, Les Amís). No independent study measuring friend-app "misuse" for dating, or whether these tactics work, turned up.

### Cited Findings
- Timeleft: "Timeleft isn't a dating app." "Timeleft matches people based on personality, not photos." "Groups are matched across backgrounds, ages, and personality answers to build balanced tables." "And yes, some people who meet at Timeleft dinners end up dating, and a few have gotten engaged." "Romance happens. It isn't what the table is set up for." Dinner groups are 4 to 6. — [Timeleft blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/is-timeleft-a-dating-app/)
- TechCrunch says Timeleft matches users "based on age, gender, and personality". Timeleft's own blog doesn't explicitly mention gender balancing (possible discrepancy) — [TechCrunch, 2026-04-05](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/) vs [Timeleft blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/is-timeleft-a-dating-app/)
- 222 co-founder: "All 222 experiences are always in public and in a group setting, unlike most dating app meet-ups." (search snippet of TechCrunch) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3050440). A secondary source says that, "whilst not a dating app, connections can develop romantically if mutual interest exists." — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/ai-powered-offline-meetup-platform-222-raises-10-1m-series-a). 222 runs vetting before notifying selected participants — [TechCrunch, 2026-04-05](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/). The 222 homepage itself lists a "blind date" format — [222.place](https://www.222.place/)
- TechCrunch says friend apps "clearly indicate that everyone using the app is looking for the same thing — friendship, not romance." It describes no specific anti-dating feature — [TechCrunch, 2026-04-05](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/)
- Women/LGBTQ+-only spaces: Les Amís is "a friendship app tailored for women, transgender, and LGBTQ+ individuals" ($70 NYC / €55 Amsterdam membership) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/). Peanut uses selfie verification and the line "A safe space for women only" (search snippet) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1178656034). Hey! VINA is "for (girl) Friends" — [heyvina.com](https://www.heyvina.com/)
- ID verification: Synchrony (neurodivergent adults) requires "two-step identity verification" with a trusted person plus photo ID. Mmotion (NYC) requires an application — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/)
- Bumble BFF originally showed only same-gender profiles in BFF mode (search snippet) — [TechCrunch, 2016-03-04](https://techcrunch.com/2016/03/04/bumble-launches-bff-a-feature-to-find-new-friends). It runs on the same swipe-and-match mechanics as dating Bumble — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/26/bumble-is-a-launching-a-separate-bff-app-for-friend-friending). A user essay describes BFF as working "like a dating app" — [Substack, "Friend Dating"](https://runawayart.substack.com/p/friend-dating) (anecdote)
- Market size signal: per Appfigures, over a dozen local friendship apps generated about $16M in US consumer spending, with about 4.3M downloads (the article's year reference is ambiguous) — [TechCrunch, 2026-04-05](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/)
- Broader stigma: a US SurveyMonkey poll found more than half of 18 to 24-year-olds see dating apps as platforms for casual hookups, and about 56% of adults view dating apps negatively — [SurveyMonkey](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/dating-apps-and-sites-are-almost-as-common-as-they-are-disliked/)

### Inferences
- Trimegos's mechanics (minimum three, public places, no 1:1 DMs, chat unlocks only when the group fills, photos hidden until unlock, crew vote, admin-approved photos, chat moderation) go further structurally than any product found. Timeleft relies on curation plus messaging. Trimegos can state its rules as product facts rather than promises.
- The honest Timeleft line ("Romance happens. It isn't what the table is set up for.") is a useful model. Denying all romance outright is less believable than saying the format isn't built for it.
- Gender-segregated spaces are the most explicit anti-dating tactic in the market. Trimegos's `gender_pref` audience filter can be framed the same way ("women-only gigs").

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study or company data on the share of friend-app users seeking romance, or on whether group formats or verification reduce it. Reddit threads weren't retrieved.
- Bumble's 2026 rule changes (Wikipedia mentions an August 2026 change letting men initiate) are unverified and concern the dating app, not BFF.

## 4. Sri Lankan language and culture: youth advertising, friend-group words, connotations, dating-app perceptions

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin. "Machan" (Sinhala, also Sri Lankan Tamil "macha/machan") is the well-documented casual word for a friend ("bro"). Sri Lankan coverage of dating apps reports stigma, trust issues and parental approval. That supports a strongly non-dating, group-and-public message. I found no sourced Singlish campaign from Dialog, PickMe or Kotmale, and nothing on local connotations of "meetup" or "hookup".

### Cited Findings
- "Machan" (මචං) is a casual Sinhala term for friend or peer, glossed as "brother" or "bro". Spelling in sources is "machan", not "machang" — [Wikipedia: Sinhala slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_slang), [Lakpura: Sinhala slang](https://www.lakpura.com/pages/sinhala-slang), [Magpie & Maple review (Machan Kitchen)](https://magpieandmaple.substack.com/p/review-machan-kitchen). In Tamil, "machan" literally means brother-in-law but is commonly used for a friend — [Wikipedia: Loanwords in Sri Lankan Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanwords_in_Sri_Lankan_Tamil). Sri Lankan English reference: [Groundviews A-Z of Sri Lankan English](https://groundviews.org/2010/12/09/a-z-of-sri-lankan-english-g-is-for-gode)
- Language mix: an industry guide estimates about 75% of the country consumes content in Sinhala and about 15% in Tamil, with the urban professional segment skewing English. It advises rewriting campaigns per language rather than translating — [Advertising Sri Lanka](https://advertisingsrilanka.lk/blog/brand-awareness-campaigns-sri-lanka) (industry vendor blog; no methodology given)
- A Sri Lankan marketing piece says AI tools cannot replicate "Singlish" humour or the cultural nods that make a campaign go viral in Colombo (opinion) — [Hype Sri Lanka](https://www.hypesrilanka.com/chatgpt-5-in-sri-lanka/)
- Youth-targeted examples found: a Munchee ad using rap music — [LBB Online "High Five: Sri Lanka"](https://lbbonline.com/news/high-five-sri-lanka-2); Dialog "Youth Can" with the Ministry of Youth & Sports (ages 14 to 35; a talent programme, not an ad) — [The Island](https://island.lk/dialog-partners-ministry-of-youth-sports-to-empower-youth-of-sri-lanka-with-youth-can/)
- Dating-app perceptions in Sri Lanka (qualitative only): Roar Media says Sri Lanka-specific stats "proved difficult to obtain", reports that few Tinder matches go beyond exchanging numbers, and notes "a lot of trust issues" (search snippet; page fetch 404'd) — [Roar Media archive](https://archive.roar.media/english/life/srilanka-life/tinder-in-sri-lanka-swipe-left-or-right). A personal essay says "there is a stigma surrounding these apps in a country like Sri Lanka" and describes "raised eyebrows, double takes and subtle leers" (search snippet) — [Pulse.lk](https://pulse.lk/everythingelse/dating-apps-through-the-eyes-of-a-hopeless-romantic-in-lanka/). A psychologist quoted in The Juggernaut says young people still find parental approval "very, very important" (search snippet) — [The Juggernaut](https://www.thejuggernaut.com/dating-in-sri-lanka)

### Inferences
- Since dating apps carry stigma and trust problems in Sri Lanka, Trimegos benefits from leading with group plus public plus activity, so that a user can tell parents "I'm playing futsal with a crew" rather than "I met someone on an app". The "not a dating app" message probably matters more locally than in Western markets. It should show up in the format and the rules, not as a defensive headline.
- "Crew" (already in the headline) is neutral English that urban Colombo audiences will understand. "Machan" is warm and recognisable but gendered/male-coded in usage ("bro"). Testing it in a secondary line or social copy is safer than putting it in the brand headline.
- "Gang" and "batch" are commonly used in Sri Lankan English ("batch" for school or university cohorts), but I found no source confirming this. Treat it as a hypothesis for user interviews.

### Gaps
- No sourced Singlish/Tanglish ad examples from Dialog, PickMe or Kotmale. LMD, Daily FT marketing columns and Adeeb weren't surfaced by search.
- No source on Sri Lankan connotations of "meetup" or "hookup". Validate in user interviews.
- No quantitative Sri Lankan survey on dating-app use or perception. The Roar, Pulse and Juggernaut pieces are anecdotal and several years old.
- No source for "gang", "batch" or "crew" usage frequency among Sri Lankan youth.

## 5. Positioning frameworks

### Takeaway
April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* has five components in a set order: competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, best-fit customers, market category (some versions add relevant trends). Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* template is "For [target customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], [product] [differentiator]." Most sources found are secondary summaries.

### Cited Findings
- Dunford: competitive alternatives come first ("What would customers do if your product didn't exist? That's usually a spreadsheet, or a manual process, or just doing nothing"). Attributes are "only 'unique' when compared with competitive alternatives". Value depends on the attributes. Market category is "the context for your product. It sets up buyer expectations." Order matters: "get the order wrong and the whole thing collapses." — [Gainsight summary](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/product-positioning-strategy-in-economic-volatility-lessons-from-the-obviously-awesome-april-dunford/), [Miro template](https://miro.com/templates/april-dunfords-positioning-template/), [Book highlights (Substack)](https://seekingnorthbyrohit.substack.com/p/book-highlights-obviously-awesome), [STFO podcast](https://stfo.io/podcast/april-dunford-product-positioning), [The Marketing Meetup](https://themarketingmeetup.com/blog/positioning-april-dunford/)
- Moore: a two-part template, a value proposition (target customer, need, product name, category, benefit) plus a differentiation statement ("Unlike…") — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/product-positioning-statement/), [Dean Peters PM skills](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/positioning-statement). Many search results were spammy template mirrors; verify exact wording against the book.

### Inferences
- For Trimegos, the Dunford competitive alternatives are probably WhatsApp groups and friends-of-friends, Facebook groups and Instagram pages for futsal/hiking, Playo-style venue booking, Meetup, dating apps used "for friends", and doing nothing. Unique attributes against those: open seats on someone's real plan, minimum three, public places, photos and chat unlock only when the group is full, an age/gender audience filter, moderation. Value: "turn a plan into a full table without asking around, with safety built into the format".
- Example Moore draft (for the writer to refine, not sourced): "For young adults in Colombo whose plans fall through for lack of people, Trimegos is a group-plans app that fills open spots with verified locals in public places. Unlike dating apps or WhatsApp groups, every gig is three or more people, and chat and photos only open once the group is full."

### Gaps
- Didn't access the primary text of either book. Wording comes from secondary summaries.

## 6. B2B value propositions of venue-partner programmes

### Takeaway
Venue programmes promise three things: (1) fill unused capacity or off-peak slots, (2) new customers who haven't visited before, and (3) zero setup cost or effort. Timeleft adds that it pays nothing and charges nothing, so venues earn only from what guests order. Its weak point, which it admits itself, is that venues drop out when groups don't spend.

### Cited Findings
- Timeleft venue partners: it looks for "diverse menus, convenient locations, high standards, and a welcoming atmosphere". Promised: "increased visibility, a steady flow of engaged guests, media features" and handled logistics. Venues don't pay Timeleft and Timeleft doesn't pay venues; the revenue is guest orders. Its help centre notes that when groups don't order, venues ask to stop hosting (search snippet; the help-centre fetch returned 403) — [Timeleft help: Partnering with Timeleft (Restaurants & Bars)](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/14236028247836-Partnering-with-Timeleft-Restaurants-Bars), [Do I need to buy food or drinks?](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/23810407006236-Do-I-Need-to-Buy-Food-or-Drinks-at-My-Timeleft-Event). The Timeleft homepage frames venues as "a bar we've booked" and "a café we've picked" — [timeleft.com](https://timeleft.com/)
- ClassPass: it pitches monetising inventory that would otherwise go unbooked (off-peak, new classes). It cites the average studio filling only 37% of capacity directly, and says "94% of ClassPass users are brand new to the venues they visit" (ClassPass/Mindbody data, self-reported). No cost to join. SmartSpot predicts no-shows; SmartRate dynamic pricing gave U.S. partners about 20% higher payouts in 2024 (self-reported) (search snippets) — [ClassPass: How ClassPass Supports Partners](https://classpass.com/blog/how-classpass-supports-partners/), [ClassPass partners: Is ClassPass worth it](https://classpass.com/partners/blog/classpass-worth-it-studios-gyms). A critic says it "cannot build the kind of client loyalty that drives recurring revenue" — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/is-classpass-good-for-fitness-studios/)
- Playo: the homepage has only a "Partner with us" link plus the "5,000+ venues across 100+ cities" reach claim. No partner benefits are visible — [playo.co](https://playo.co/)

### Inferences
- Trimegos's venue pitch could borrow the ClassPass framing (fill quiet slots such as weekday-evening futsal courts and off-peak café tables) and the Timeleft zero-fee model, while avoiding Timeleft's no-spend problem. Each gig is a pre-formed group of 3+ that has already committed to a time, which works as a booking.
- The Sri Lankan futsal court build-out (Cabinet-approved PPP courts) points to growing court inventory that needs filling. That is a concrete B2B wedge.

### Gaps
- No Groupon merchant value proposition was retrieved.
- Playo's /partner-with-us page wasn't fetched. Its venue promises (booking management, payouts) are unverified.
- Timeleft's partner page couldn't be read in full (404/403). Commission terms are unknown.
- No Sri Lankan venue-aggregator examples (local futsal booking platforms) were researched.
